About

Hi! I'm a PhD candidate at the University of California, Santa Barbara where I study attention and thought with Dr. Barry Giesbrecht and Dr. Jonathan Schooler. I also collaborate with researchers at NASA Ames Research Center in the Human–Computer Interaction Group, where I study human–machine teaming in deep space mission analogs. My work has been generously supported by the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship, and commended by the NASA Ames Honor Award and the APA Anastasi Award.

Research areas

How do attention, perception, and memory interact?

  • How can we track attentional fluctuations using real-time, closed-loop designs? (Shelat et al., 2024)
  • How does attention at encoding determine the fate of visual memories? (Shelat et al., 2026)
  • What happens when attention disengages from perceptual input? (Shelat & Giesbrecht, 2025)
  • How does capture vary across sustained attentional states? (Shelat & Giesbrecht, 2026)

What’s happening in our minds?

  • Can language let us infer underlying mental states like workload? (Shelat et al., 2024)
  • How might consciousness react to the ways we study consciousness? (Garg, Shelat et al., 2025)
  • How can we analyze experiential reports with more rigor? (Shelat et al., submitted)
  • When does mind-wandering hurt and when does it help? (Shelat & Schooler, 2026)
  • How do people experience their thoughts? (Marome*, Shelat* et al., 2025)

How do we interact with machines?

  • Does trust in software evolve over continual use in extreme environments? (Shelat et al., 2026)
  • How can we design better machines to enhance human performance? (Karasinski, Shelat et al., 2025)
  • How might partial automation reduce task demands? (Zheng, Shelat et al., 2023)
  • How do we make more usable technologies? (Shelat et al., 2025)
